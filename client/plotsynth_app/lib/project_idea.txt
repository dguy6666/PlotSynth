Part 1: Finding facts, contradictions, and forgotten details

For this stage, you are breaking the text down and inspecting it. The correct terms are:
• Story Auditing / Developmental Editing: This is the professional term in publishing. It means looking at the big picture of a manuscript to find plot holes, character inconsistencies, and structural issues.
• Cross-examining / Fact-checking the drafts: Comparing your drafts against each other to find where the details don't match (e.g., a character having blue eyes in Draft 1 but green eyes in Draft 2).
• Parsing / Dissecting: Breaking the story down scene-by-scene to extract the core facts and timeline.

Part 2: Using those findings to fix the story

For this stage, you are putting things back together. While synthesize can work here (because you are blending the best parts of different drafts into one final version), authors and editors usually use these more specific creative writing terms:
• Reconciling: This is the perfect word for fixing contradictions. To "reconcile the drafts" means you take two conflicting facts and fix them so they finally make sense together.
• Consolidating / Merging: Taking the forgotten details and the best elements from various drafts and combining them into a single, master draft.
• Synthesizing: You can use this here if you mean: "I am synthesizing my notes and drafts into a cohesive final story."
• Polishing / Refining: Smoothing out the inconsistencies so the final narrative flows perfectly without any bumps.

The Distinction Between Outline (Truth) and Story Dialogue (Lies)
This insight is absolute gold for your software engineering project.

What you just realized is one of the most sophisticated challenges in literary Natural Language Processing, and presenting it to your college examiners will make you look like a senior software architect:

Layer 1: The Outline = "Authorial Ground Truth" (100% Fact)
In the Outline, the author speaks as the omniscient creator.

There are no lies or unreliable narrators here: when you write "The Fitin kingdom is infested with thieves and serial killers", the system accepts it as absolute, immutable Ground Truth.

Layer 2: The Manuscript (Story) = "Objective Prose vs. Subjective Dialogue"
When you audit a written chapter, the NLP engine must distinguish between two different types of text:

Type A — Objective Narrator Text (Audited Strictly):

Example: "Daniel walked through the peaceful, crime-free streets of Fitin."

Result: 🔴 Contradiction Alert! The third-person narrator just stated a fact that violates the established Lore in your database.

Type B — Character Dialogue in Quotes (Subjective / Can Lie):

Example: A shady merchant says to Daniel: "Welcome to Fitin, my boy! A lovely, safe kingdom with no thieves at all."

Result: 🟢 No Contradiction! The engine recognizes this is character speech inside quotation marks ("..."). Characters can lie, spread rumors, be deceived, or use sarcasm. That is dramatic irony, not a plot hole!
